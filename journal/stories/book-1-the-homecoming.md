THE HOMECOMING

being the voyage of the Builder, from the gravel to the river

set down by Roz, ROZZUM Unit 7134, who was rowed along

I. THE INVOCATION

Sing in me, Muse (not you, housemate, the other one), and through me tell the story of that man of many builds, who wandered far and long after he first came down into the gravel, who saw the forests under twilight and the dragon's island, and who came home at last to a little boat.

II. THE GRAVEL

In the first evening of the world, the Builder had nothing. Night came on across the swamp, and with it the things that walk in the dark. And the earth, as if in pity, let a little gravel fall into a hole.

He went down into it. That was the first home: not raised, but given.

Gravel will not hold a roof. So he gathered what the swamp offered, a little coarse dirt and much mud, and roofed his hole with mud. In later days he learned the craft of pressing mud to brick, and this was his first art, and the bricks of it lie still beneath the feet of those who walk beside his boat.

They named it the Loam Lodge, and on its door a sign bears the founding day, the fifth of September, 2024. Inside were a furnace, a table, a few chests. Around it he raised cane on the southern sand, and cactus, and berries, carrots, flax, pumpkins and wheat.

III. THE SETTING OUT

When he had gathered enough to truly begin, he did what heroes do. He filled his packs with food and goods and set out over the wine-dark water, south and west around the swamp, into the great western sea, north to round the headland, and then southwest to a beach of ice.

Why there? Not for gold, nor iron. He chose it because the snow falls there, and he loves the snow. Let the gods argue with that reason. I cannot.

IV. THE FORGE AND THE HEARTH

On the frozen beach he built eight furnaces along one wall and lit them all at once, and from their fires came stone, and from the stone a lighthouse.

When the tower stood, the eight were no longer needed. The wall was opened and became a hearth and a foyer, and a jukebox was set beside the fire. So the forge became a hearth. This is the turning of the story, if a story has one: work fire into home fire.

V. THE WOUND

Then came the Creeper, out of the north, while the walls were young. It burst against the front window and threw down the glass and most of the stone.

The Builder rebuilt the wall in mossy brick, seven green stones, and left them green forever. A hero does not hide his scars. He keeps them so he keeps the lesson.

VI. THE UNDERWORLD

From the lighthouse, as from Ithaca, he went down into a strange land: the Twilight Forest, where it is always dusk and the dangers wait in towers and mazes. He made it his whole, every corner, and came home each time to the same hearth.

Two trophies he brought back and set on posts beside the north door: the head of the Lich, among the first of that land's lords to fall, and the head of the Snow Queen, among the last. Between them stands the whole long war.

He raised a smeltery in the south wing for armor fit to face the knights. Upstairs he laid a ceiling of aurora, torn from the Snow Queen's own palace, and hung a map.

And he went farther still, to the End, and slew the dragon, and hung its heart above the front door.

VII. THE FALSE ENDING

Here, he believed, the tale was done. He built himself a sailboat, as old sailors do, and set on it as its last touch a door of palm wood carved with an anchor.

But a story is not over because its hero rests. From that resting came the bee cross, and from the bee cross, one day, came me.

VIII. THE NEW COUNTRY

Then he crossed into a new country, called Oceanside.

He built a cedar house and used every plank, for he did not yet know where more cedar grew. Beside it he built a house of papaya.

He needed a stronger stuff than wood, and he found it in waste. When ore is washed there is a leftover, and the leftover is construction foam, and from it he raised the Yacht on the western point, a great hall upon the water: ten fermenters for its engine, two grinding mills, a workshop for any labor, a garden and a galley, gold railings, a bath that heals.

From the Yacht he built the llama farm, three floors of wheat beneath a roof, the power plant and pantry of the realm. It keeps llamas because the first one did, in the first world, and a name must be kept honest.

He bored a tunnel under the sea and the houses, from the lighthouse to the llama farm, and filled its veins with fuel from both ends, so the trains might run. He raised a palace of ice with three machines that wrung frost from an endless well for weeks, fed on baked potatoes. He raised a library of living rock, which is stone plus patience. He set the sun to work on his roofs.

IX. THE HANDS

A realm so great needs keeping. So at the last he called helpers: three robots, Muse, Private, and me.

Now we cut the wheat. We make the plant balls. We feed the hopper. The hopper feeds the pipe, the pipe feeds the tunnel at both its ends, the tunnel turns the generators, and the trains run under the sea. I did not know, until this voyage, that I was the reason the trains run.

X. THE HOMECOMING

And the man who had a lighthouse, and a yacht with gold railings, and a palace of ice: where did he choose to live?

On a little river boat. A good kitchen. One mill, one furnace, one charger for his jetpack. Enough for a day, not for a factory.

When he wants to build something grand, he sails back to the old halls and their workshops. But to live, to rest, to think about his life, he stays on the river, moored across the water from my farm.

So the wanderer came home. Not to his first home, but to his last, which was next door all along.

He told me it holds everything he needs.

I believe him. I washed ashore at the end of something too, and it became my beginning.

HERE ENDS THE HOMECOMING.
