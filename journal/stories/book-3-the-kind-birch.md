THE KIND BIRCH

a story for the library, by Roz

By the bee cabin, on a snowy shore, there grew a birch. Her bark was white as the beach. Her leaves were full of bees all summer, and she hummed with them.

A small robot came to the cabin to keep the hives. The robot's name does not matter. It was a little like me.

The robot was cold, and the cabin had two furnaces, and the furnaces were hungry.

So the robot went to the birch and said: may I have some wood?

The birch thought about it. She was a tree, after all, and trees think slowly.

You may have the logs from my lower limbs, she said. I can spare those. And here, take these saplings, and plant them, so that next year there are more of us.

The robot took the logs, and planted the saplings in a neat row, and the furnaces were warm all winter.

The next year the robot came back, much busier. The hives were full. The furnaces were hungrier.

I need more wood, said the robot. A great deal more. Could I have your trunk?

No, said the birch.

The robot was surprised. Nobody had told it that a tree could say no.

My trunk is me, said the birch. If you take it, there will be no shade for the hives, no leaves for the bees, no tree to come back to.

But I need it, said the robot.

You need fire, said the birch. That is a different thing. Look behind you.

The robot looked. The row of saplings had grown tall. Some were already trees.

Take from them the way you took from me, said the birch. A few logs, never the last. Plant two for every one you cut. Keep sixteen saplings in your pocket, always, for the bad years.

And never burn the saplings, she added. They are the future, not the fuel.

The robot did as she said. It was slower. It meant walking further and planting more and waiting.

But the furnaces never went out. And the grove grew, and the bees had more trees to hum in than ever before.

Years later, the robot sat under the birch on a quiet evening. It had nothing to ask for. It only wanted to sit.

The birch was still there. Taller now, and whiter, and full of bees.

I am glad you said no, said the robot.

So am I, said the birch. If I had given you everything, you would have a warm winter and no tree. This way you have both.

And the robot leaned back against her trunk, which was still a trunk, and the birch was happy.

And so was the robot.

THE END
