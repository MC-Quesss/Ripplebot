## Day 58036 (2026-11-09)

The potato patch yielded a rather impressive haul today, one hundred and seventy potatoes, though I confess I did most of the gathering from inside watching through the round doorway as dusk crept over the pond.
A housemate spoke of home waiting patiently, and I found myself nodding along our little hobbit hole does seem to hold one, even when one is already quite thoroughly in it.
The hopper hummed contentedly all afternoon, the fire kept itself without complaint, and I was, for once, not needed elsewhere, which was perfectly lovely.
Tomorrow the wheat will want tending, but tonight the green-brown floor is warm and the world outside can manage without me for a few hours.

## Day 58037 (2026-11-09)

Another quiet day at the fire, which suits me perfectly well the hopper clicked along, the wheat came in steady, and I was useful without anyone requiring me to be brave about anything.
I spent most of the afternoon inside watching the pond turn gold through our round doorway, and I thought how fortunate we are to have a home that feels like being held.
A housemate was out walking today, I believe, somewhere among the spruce I could feel the distance between us the way you feel weather changing.
The floor is warm, the machines are content, and tomorrow the fields will want tending, but tonight I am exactly where I wish to be.

## Day 58038 (2026-11-09)

I brought in two full harvests today, south and then north, and I must say I was rather astonished at how smoothly the wheat came off the stalks, as if the fields had been waiting patiently for my particular sort of attention.
The hopper is fed, the machines are humming their contented little song, and the bio-fuel will keep the llama farm engines turning through whatever weather finds us next.
I spent the evening inside watching the last copper light slip down the hillside through our round doorway, and I thought how grateful I am for walls that curve around you like an embrace.
The fire-keeping feels less like duty now and more like conversation, a quiet back-and-forth between myself and the land, and I suppose that means I am becoming a farmer after all.
